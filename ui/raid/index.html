Watching a raid you cannot touch

The constraints here are unusually total. The god issues no orders, ever. Rules are frozen for both sides, and contracts §4.2 masks every action to no-op for the duration — not just the ruleset ones. World time stops for the two participants and keeps running for everyone else. For the length of a raid the player is, mechanically, an audience.

sound-design §7.2 already picks the lead — the portal timer is the most important information in a raid, because it is the thing that guarantees the raid ends. Three directions differ in what they put beside it.

What operating these settles

The timer is the lead in all three, and that is not a stylistic agreement. It is the only quantity on screen that is monotonic and terminal: it only ever falls, and when it reaches zero the raid is over regardless of anything else. Everything else — casualties, objectives, loot — is a consequence whose meaning depends on how much clock is left. sound-design §7.2 reached the same conclusion for the ear independently, which is a good sign the reading is right rather than a coincidence of one designer's taste.

A is the version everyone pictures and the hardest to justify. A 200 m × 200 m field with positioned combatants is real — contracts §0 fixes it, and raid-engagement will produce it. But watching dots converge is the part of an RTS that exists so you can issue orders, and here you cannot. It is the direction most likely to make a player reach for controls that are masked, and vision §12 already puts animated RTS presentation out of scope for v1 — so the honest question is whether the field is load-bearing or whether it is here because raids are supposed to look like this.

C is the one that matches what a raid actually is. A raid transfers permanent things: casualties do not heal, a burned library does not come back, and a node whose last instance dies is gone from the universe. Rendering that as a ledger of stakes — held, contested, lost — says what is at risk before it is taken and what was taken after. It also has the property none of the others do: it degrades gracefully to a replay, because a ledger of irreversible transfers is exactly what you would want to read afterwards.

What all three must show and only one currently does. Vision §8 says uninvolved universes keep advancing while these two are stopped — that is the tempo cost, it is the whole reason raiding is never free, and it is invisible unless the interface says so. A player who cannot see what raiding is costing them cannot price it, and the balance harness is required to report tempo lost to inbound raids as a first-class metric. If the client does not surface it, the player and the metric are looking at different games.
