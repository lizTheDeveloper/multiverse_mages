Every spell gets a moment in the world

A raid, watched from inside the universe being raided. Agency is front-loaded: you spend the raid verb set under a clock, intervene less and less sharply once the two sides touch, and by the end you have earned the right to only watch. You cannot stop the clock — you can always look at what already happened. Click any mage to inspect her; scrub the record back to any tick and the engagement keeps running underneath, because rewind is to view, never to act. Every spell drawn on the field is a node in packages/content/data dispatched through the seven effect primitives arbitration.ts knows.

What drives this, and what it is not

realBaked from content

  • The twelve v1 cells — cell.json's "v1": true flag, which is exactly contracts §2.2's 3-technique × 4-form rectangle: intellego / perdo / rego over limen / mentem / nomen / terram.
  • All 51 nodes in those cells, with their real ids, display names, tiers, and every engagement-scale effect at its authored magnitude and duration.
  • Every stacking rule and cap from primitive.json: ward and concealment multiplicative-on-remainder at fp(922) and fp(870), blink and knowledge-steal by max, area-denial and summon additive.
  • Every constant in raid-constant.json — extent, terrain chances, deployment depth, movement, cast and theft range, hit points, cast vigor, portal stability and its decay, the withdraw margin, the victory fraction.

mirroredCopied from arbitration.ts

  • The seven-primitive dispatch of CastArbiter#effectsOf, switch arm for switch arm.
  • Both legality layers, and forbiddenCastsBlocked as a counter that could have been non-zero.
  • applyWardOnce — damage summed, then exactly one ward application, floor(raw × (1024 − ward) / 1024). Never per source.
  • The cast order: legality, then cost, then the host's cast hook, then effects. An unaffordable cast keeps its preparation.
  • Not imported. @mm/rules-raid is a TypeScript workspace with no browser build; this is a hand-copy and can drift from it.

authored hereIn no content file

  • The muster duration (300 engagement ticks), the cast cooldown, vigor regeneration, and the intent policy that picks a target and a node.
  • The playback rate, which is a viewing rate and not a rule. The engagement tick is 100 ms, so a trace played at 100 ms a tick runs 1:1 with the world. Contact and resolution are played at 400 ms a tick — 2.5 ticks a second, a quarter of engagement time — so that a cast can be read. Muster is not slowed, because its clock is the pressure. The speed control is rebased on the slow pace: 1× is the default, 4× is 1:1, 16× is for skipping ahead. Nothing about it changes what the trace decides.
  • Mage rosters and their held nodes — drawn from the seed. There is no world state behind them.
  • Objective instance counts, and therefore objective values.
  • The damage multiplier. It defaults to ×1 — the authored magnitudes; ×24 is a viewing multiplier on direct-damage and area-denial only, there to show what a world where spells decide fights would look like.
  • Line of sight is sampled along the segment, not walked with geometry.ts's supercover trace, and the endpoints' own cells are excluded from it — see finding 4.
  • Movement follows a breadth-first flow field over the 400 terrain cells, standing in for navigation.ts, which this page cannot import.

ahead of the engineDesign, not shipped code

  • Mid-raid forbidding. arbitration.ts computes the legal mask once from a frozen snapshot. The lock-don't-freeze behaviour animated here is raid-engagement.md §1, which W37 is implementing on w37/raid-playable. This is the single most believable untruth on the page, which is why it is named here.
  • Rewind is not a verb anywhere either — but it is the one thing here that is nearly free. packages/sim-core already ships deterministic replay, versioned snapshots and golden replay fixtures, and step(state, actions, rng) → state is pure: no wall-clock reads, no Math.random, no floating point in the rules path. A raid replay is a capability to expose, not a feature to build — seed plus action log re-runs an engagement identically, which is exactly the property the golden fixtures assert on every commit. This page generates its engagement from a seed, so it already holds the record. Rewinding here keeps no snapshots at all: it re-runs the engagement into a copy from the seed and an append-only log of every directive and the tick it landed on. A whole raid re-runs in about 7 ms, half a frame, which is why that is affordable. The live state is never touched, so looking back cannot desync anything. It is one of the few places where this prototype's honest limitation and the engine's actual capability point the same way — and in the real game, seed plus action log is also small enough to hand to someone else, which makes a raid a shareable artifact for disputes and study.
  • What was here before, and is now deleted. Until this pass, clicking a mage stopped the action and charged for it — 25 favor on a rising ladder, a raider counter-bid of 20 Vis on its own ladder, a 40-Vis floor and 2.6 s of deliberation. raid-engagement.md §11 supersedes that outright, and the replacement needs no currency, no escalation rule and no opponent model. The scaffolding pause button went with it: a page claiming the clock cannot be stopped must not ship a button that stops it. You can freeze the record you are reading; you can never freeze the engagement.
  • Vis and exposure exist in no package. §3 proposals, drawn as proposals. Vis is also a spec collision — economy spec §250 forbids a fourth resource. With the bid gone, Vis is spent on casting and nothing else.
  • The raid verb set no longer "exists in no package" — and this page's four are not it. w37/raid-playable's raid-constant.json now carries six verb costs: verb-forbid, verb-permit, verb-levy, verb-ward, verb-mend and verb-conceal — four for the defender and two for the attacker. None of them is in this tree, and the four drawn here are an older, different set. One word covered two mechanics, so this page renamed its own rather than collide: w37's levy is one detachment out of a cohort that does not fight — its gloss says a second soldier detachment "could never fire", because the opening deployment already commits every one a soldier cohort can field — and this page's was a detachment that walks up and fights. It is now call up the reserve. The gap is named, not resolved: w37/raid-playable is unmerged, and guessing at its semantics would be worse than the collision was. Read the verbs here as a sketch that has been overtaken, not as the shipped set.
  • The god still issues no orders. vision §7 is not repealed. Every verb here is permit, forbid, or favor spent on a standing condition; nothing targets a combatant.
  • Open questions left open. The muster clock runs on engagement time because something had to be picked (§6.5). Exposure counts what was observed, not what was learned (§6.6).

Five things this prototype found, which is what a prototype is for

  1. direct-damage is the one engagement primitive the authored magnitudes leave inert. Measured over twenty seeds at the authored magnitudes — the ×1 default — of every hit point removed in a raid: area-denial 49.7%, summoned servants and soldier detachments 48.4%, and direct-damage 1.9%. The arithmetic is visible inside a single node. pm-the-empty-room, tier 5, the deepest working in the v1 rectangle, carries direct-damage: 768 — fp(0.75) against a mage's fp(64) of hit points — and area-denial: 192 for 40 ticks, which is fp(7.5): the same node's field does ten times its own bolt. Meanwhile summon-damage is fp(2) per tick. A per-cast primitive is competing with per-tick ones and losing by two orders of magnitude. Every node is tuningStatus: untuned, so this is a gap waiting on the balance harness rather than a bug — but it is exactly the gap winRateByPrimitive exists to catch, and it is catchable now.
  2. Every damaging node in the v1 rectangle is Perdo. All fourteen of them. Which makes raid-engagement.md §1's sharpest case sharper than it says, and different in kind: forbidding Perdo mid-raid does not quiet the field, it changes what the war is made of. Forbid all four Perdo cells and, over the same twenty seeds, the share of damage done by summons and soldiers goes from 48.4% to 87.2% while cast volume barely moves — 119 casts a raid becomes 103, because mages fall back to the Rego workings they can still use and the field fills with wards, blinks and called servants instead. forbiddenCastsBlocked comes off zero immediately and reaches about 58 a raid, every one of them your own defender reaching for something you took away. You have not stopped the war; you have traded a war of unmaking for a war of summoned servants, and you cannot trade back. Try it: forbid the whole Perdo row and watch the ledger.
  3. The portal timer, which sound-design §7.2 calls the most important information in a raid, currently decides nothing. Median contact lasts 162 engagement ticks against a portal authored to stand for 3,000. Across twenty seeds not one raid reached withdraw-stability-margin, so the stranded-raider rule — the number most likely to need softening, by its own gloss — never fired once. Either combatants are far too fragile for the portal's lifetime or the portal is authored for a much longer engagement than the rest of the constants produce, and those are different fixes.
  4. The authored terrain is harsher about sight than about ground, and both bite. terrain-blocking-chance is fp(154) on top of an 8% impassable roll, so a sight-blocker sits in about a fifth of the 400 cells and a fp(50) cast crosses enough of them that most long shots have no line: closing is the dominant move, and that falls out of the constants rather than out of any policy here. Two consequences bit this page hard enough to be worth passing on. A supercover trace must exclude the endpoints' own cells, or two enemies who walk into the same wood cannot see each other, neither can cast, and both stand there until the portal shuts — which is precisely what happened here, in four seeds out of sixteen, and looked exactly like a pathfinding bug. And 8% impassable is enough to make concave pockets: a greedy step oscillates in front of one for the rest of the raid, so this page ended up with a breadth-first flow field. That is an argument for plugging navigation.ts in rather than approximating it.
  5. Two content files disagree about the summon cap. primitive.json caps summon at count-per-side: 8; raid-constant.json's max-summons-per-side is 16. This page uses 16 as the alive-cap and 8 as the per-stack cap, which is one reading of two, and nothing appears to enforce either against the other.
