The verbs whose consequence is not now

Bless and assign-role resolve on the tick you press them. These four do not. A university costs 22,528 favor across five payments and contributes nothing until the last one. An encouragement decays to nothing in 64 months. Changing tradition zeroes your pool. Opening a portal stops world time for both universes while everyone else keeps running.

Every other verb the interface has is a purchase; these are commitments. Move the worship tier — the reach direction is where they stop being a price question.

What operating these settles

One of these actions is not expensive — it is out of reach, and only the third direction can say so. change-tradition costs 65,536 against a cap of 20,480 + tier × 10,240, so it exceeds what a universe can hold at every tier below the highest. A price list renders that as a big number the player might save toward, and they cannot: the pool physically cannot get there. That is the difference between "expensive" and "not yet a verb you have", and the cost table's own gloss says it was priced that way deliberately.

The horizon is the only direction that shows the dead interval. A university returns nothing — not reduced worship, not partial capacity — until buildProgress reaches fp(1024), which is four fundings after the founding. Four of the five payments buy no observable change whatever, and a player who cannot see that will read the first three as broken. The same shape appears inverted on encouragement, which pays immediately and is gone in 64 months.

Cost per tick of effect is the comparison nobody can make, and every direction here should probably show it. Bless is 2,048 for 120 ticks — about 17 favor a tick. Encourage is 1,024 for an emphasis that decays over 64 — about 16 a tick, and pointed at a cell rather than a person. Those two are near-identical purchases at very different face prices, and no interface that shows only the face price will ever let a player notice.

What is missing from all three. open-portal's real price is tempo, not favor — vision §8 makes the world-time pause the reason raiding is never free, and 16,384 is explicitly the second thing about it per the cost gloss. None of these directions can show tempo cost, because that is a number about the universes you are not looking at. Since W8 landed raids this stopped being hypothetical: raids now fire, so the tempo is now actually being spent and still has no home in the interface.
