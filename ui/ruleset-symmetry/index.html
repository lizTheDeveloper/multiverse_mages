Multiverse Mages · design pillar #1 · vision §3 · two more directions

The ruleset, read as a symmetry

ui/ruleset/ answers which cells are live. Neither of these does. They answer the question vision §3 actually poses:

The host universe's ruleset governs all magic cast inside it, for both attacker and defender.

So permitting a cell arms your mages and anyone who invades you. The strategic content of the entire game is in that sentence, and a grid of toggles shows it nowhere — a permitted cell looks identical whether your civilization has spent two centuries in it or has never held a single node there. The second kind is a pure gift to an attacker, and nothing currently says so.

What operating these settles

The denial play is invisible in a grid and obvious in a ledger. Vision §3 says prohibiting is a real strategic option rather than a penalty, and the reason is that a cell you permit but hold no knowledge in can only ever be used against you. Direction B makes that a standing count on screen. Forbid a technique your civilization never entered and the number goes down with no cost to you at all — which is the denial play, stated as arithmetic rather than as a paragraph in a design document.

Staging is the only way to show a toggle's real cost. Permitting Perdo lights fourteen cells at once. Forbidding Intellego strands everything downstream of it, because a dormant instance cannot satisfy a prerequisite — direction C walks the real prerequisite graph and reports orphaned nodes separately from cells lost, which are very different numbers. A live toggle cannot do this: by the time you see the consequence you have already paid 8,192 favor for it.

What neither direction can do honestly, and it is the same gap. The right-hand ledger asks what an invader would know, and the answer depends on a universe you have never seen. In live PvP that is genuinely unknown; in Monte Carlo it is knowable and would be cheating to show. Both directions here fall back on what any universe could know — the reachable set under this ruleset — which is an upper bound rather than a prediction. That is the honest version, and it is weaker than the interface appears to promise. Worth deciding deliberately rather than by whichever approximation ships.

Three directions now exist for pillar #1 and they are not interchangeable. The grid is for understanding structure, the ledger for deciding policy, the staging view for pricing a specific change. That is an argument for shipping more than one of them rather than picking a winner — which is the opposite of what the other control surfaces concluded.
